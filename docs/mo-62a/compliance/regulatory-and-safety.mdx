The [Mo62A datasheet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0369/9536/7051/files/Mo_62A__V1.0_e7e46c7c-d546-41ad-bf9d-0308dbf8e339.pdf) lists 5 V / 5 A USB-C input, a 25 W maximum rating, an operating range of 0–50 °C and a storage range of −20–70 °C. The source page is shown unchanged below.

## Bench handling

- Shut down and disconnect power before connecting or moving GPIO jumpers, HATs, camera ribbons, a fan or a backup battery.
- Keep the exposed PCB on a stable, nonconductive surface; prevent loose metal or conductive tools from contacting powered circuits.
- Use 3.3 V logic on header signal pins. Power/ground pins are not GPIO, and a mechanically fitting connector does not guarantee a matching pinout.
- Use a suitable power supply and cable. Do not back-power the board through a USB-UART adapter or connect competing supplies.
- Keep cooling paths clear. Allow hot components to cool before handling them; fan blades and exposed wiring also need protection in an enclosure.
- Stop using damaged cables, connectors or batteries. Do not improvise an electrical repair while the board is powered.

For the actual connector and pin assignments, use [Hardware interfaces](../hardware-development/hardware-interfaces.mdx). For supply troubleshooting and sensor readings, use [Power & thermal](../hardware-development/power-and-thermal.mdx).

## RTC battery connection

The schematics show a two-pin SH1.0 battery connector J2, not a coin-cell holder: pin 1 is GND and pin 2 is VBAT. Obtain the approved battery assembly and confirm polarity, voltage and connector before attaching it; do not choose a bare cell solely from an older accessory description.

<a href={connectors} target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">
  <img src={connectors} alt="Original schematic sheet 17 with the RTC battery connector J2 at the lower right; pin 1 is ground and pin 2 is VBAT" width="2200" height="1556" />
</a>

Battery retention, charging behaviour and a specific replacement battery assembly were not validated in this review. Do not attempt to charge a primary cell or short its terminals. Keep small batteries away from children and use the battery supplier's handling and disposal instructions.
