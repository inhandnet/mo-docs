## Identify the board, not just the software

Record the PCB silkscreen revision, ordered SKU/memory option and any assembly label before selecting design files. Photograph the marking on your own board; an example product photograph or a Linux model string is not proof of the exact assembled revision.

`uname -r` and `/etc/os-release` describe software. The device-tree model and an RTC driver name also do not uniquely identify a hardware revision.

## What is not established

No authoritative engineering change notice, assembly/BOM change list or revision-to-serial mapping was found in the reviewed public material. Consequently, this page does not claim that every difference between the two PDFs is fitted on every board, or that accessories and images are interchangeable across all revisions.

The V1.1-named PDF has local change annotations on some sheets, but those annotations are not a complete release history. Connector references also conflict with the older SDK README: both PDFs identify the expansion header as J6 and the fan as J1. Use [Hardware interfaces](./hardware-interfaces.mdx) and verify the actual connector and pin 1.

## Before a revision-dependent change

1. Record the physical board and assembly identification.
2. Keep the exact schematic file and its hash, not just a moving branch URL.
3. Ask InHand to confirm the matching schematic/BOM, engineering changes and any required software or accessory restrictions.
4. Revalidate the affected interface, power or mechanical fit on the target revision.

For enclosure manufacture, also obtain the toleranced mechanical package; the public [dimension drawing](./mechanical.mdx) explicitly excludes production use.
