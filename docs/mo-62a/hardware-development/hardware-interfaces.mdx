Use physical pin numbers when wiring and SoC controller names when programming. A BCM-style HAT label such as `GPIO17` is not Linux GPIO line 17 on Mo62A.

:::warning[Disconnect power before wiring]
Header signals use 3.3 V logic. Do not apply 5 V to a GPIO, connect two outputs together, or assume that a mechanically compatible HAT has compatible signals and software. Locate physical pin 1 on your board before connecting anything.
:::

## Locate the board features

This original annotated Mo62A photograph identifies the connectors and controls on the pictured assembly. With the USB and Ethernet sockets on the right, the 40-pin header is along the top edge; S1 and the indicator are on the left edge, and J5 is beside the Ethernet socket. The photograph's PoE HAT label identifies the transformer-tap connection, not an on-board PoE power converter; see [Other connectors](#other-connectors).

The annotated overview identifies connector locations. Use the explicitly oriented top view below for J6 numbering, and check the pin-1 indication on your own board before wiring. Do not infer orientation from the logical diagram or an underside view.

- **S1:** the photograph locates the power button. A short-press/long-press behaviour test was not performed for this page; do not assume a reset or shutdown action from its label.
- **Indicator:** the board exposes `/sys/class/leds/red` and `/sys/class/leds/green`. Read `brightness` and `trigger` there to inspect the current software state. Linux names and trigger settings alone do not verify the observed colour or every boot/shutdown indication; see [First boot](../getting-started/first-boot.mdx) for the documented startup sequence.
- **CON1:** the left-side antenna connector serves the shared Wi-Fi/BLE antenna. Follow [Network](../mo-os/network.mdx#shared-wi-fi-and-ble-antenna) for the connection procedure.
- **Board identification:** the pictured assembly has its model/revision silkscreen below the expansion header. Check your own marking and assembly label; this reference photograph is not proof of your board revision. See [Hardware revisions](./hardware-revisions.mdx).

## 40-pin header (J6)

Both published [schematics](./schematics.mdx), sheet 17, identify the expansion header as J6 and the fan connector as J1. The older SDK README calls them J9 and J6 respectively. This page follows the schematic references; also check the `USER EXPN` marking, connector shape and pin-1 indication on your actual board. Do not select a connector by a conflicting reference alone.

### Physical orientation: top view

Orient the board as in this top/component-side reference image: the 40-pin header is at the top and the USB/Ethernet sockets are on the right. Read the columns from left to right:

- The lower row, toward the components and board centre, is **1, 3, 5, …, 39**.
- The upper row, toward the outside edge, is **2, 4, 6, …, 40**.
- In the leftmost column, pin 1 is below pin 2. In the rightmost column, pin 39 is below pin 40.

On the tested image, `gpiochip0` is `4201000.gpio` (MCU GPIO0), `gpiochip1` is `600000.gpio` (main GPIO0), and `gpiochip2` is `601000.gpio` (main GPIO1). Confirm the names on another image instead of assuming chip indices are stable.

```bash
bootcfg show
sudo gpiodetect
sudo gpioinfo -c gpiochip2
```

For driven GPIO, edge monitoring and cleanup, use the tested [GPIO & peripherals](../application-development/gpio-and-peripherals.mdx) procedures. In libgpiod v2, `gpioset --hold-period=2s` specifies a minimum hold period; it does not by itself mean that the process exits after two seconds. A GPIO output is held while the request remains open.

## Select a peripheral function

Choose a supplied entry with `bootcfg`; changing the default reboots the board. Confirm the live device-tree status after reboot. Do not enable the audio overlay with the GPIO/PWM loopback jumpers still attached.

| Function | Physical pins | Boot entry | Verification boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPIO | Unreserved signal pins | `microSD-default` | Three pairs tested in both directions |
| UART5 | 8 TX, 10 RX | `microSD-uart5` | `/dev/ttyS3`; 115200 8N1 loopback passed |
| EHRPWM0 | 33 A, 32 B | `microSD-ehrpwm0` | Pin 33 measured at 50 Hz / 25%; pin 32 not measured |
| WKUP_I2C0 | 3 SDA, 5 SCL | `microSD-i2c0` | No external I²C peripheral tested in this review |
| SPI0 | 19, 21, 23, 24, 26 | `microSD-spi0` | No SPI loopback tested in this review |
| MCASP2 audio | 12, 35, 38, 40; 3/5 control | `microSD-audio` | Requires the matching WM8960 hardware and driver |

The combined `exp-periph` overlay is present in the SDK but is not a supplied `microSD-periph` boot entry in the tested image. Follow [Device tree overlays](../system-configuration/device-tree-overlays.mdx) for the supported entries, pin conflicts and recovery.

Resolve PWM controllers through `/sys/class/pwm/pwmchip*/device`: EHRPWM0 is `23000000.pwm`; the fan uses `23010000.pwm`. Do not hard-code `pwmchip0`. Header pin 36 shares the fan's EHRPWM1 controller, so it is not an independent, already-tested PWM output.

## Camera, fan and debug UART

The CSI camera connector JP1 is a 22-pin, 0.5 mm FPC connector. The tested sensor is IMX219. Disconnect power before handling the ribbon and use the [camera connection guide and JPEG capture test](../application-development/camera-and-video.mdx).

Sheet 17 of the V1.1-named schematic is reproduced below without altering its labels. Open the image or full PDF to inspect pin numbers.

<a href={connectors} target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">
  <img src={connectors} alt="Original schematic sheet 17 showing J6 expansion, J1 fan, J4 debug UART and J2 RTC battery connectors" width="2200" height="1556" />
</a>

| Connector | Schematic pin assignment | Use |
|---|---|---|
| J1 fan, SH1.0-4p | 1: 5 V; 2: PWM; 3: GND; 4: tachometer | Use a matching fan and cable; do not rely on wire colours |
| J4 debug, SH1.0-3p | 1: board RX; 2: GND; 3: board TX | 3.3 V TTL USB-UART; 115200 baud, 8N1 |
| J2 RTC battery, SH1.0-2p | 1: GND; 2: VBAT | Verify approved battery type and plug polarity before connecting |

The debug UART is `ttyS2`, not the header's UART5 `/dev/ttyS3`. Cross adapter TX to board RX and adapter RX to board TX, and connect ground. Do not connect an RS-232 voltage interface or use the adapter's power wire to power the board. See [First boot](../getting-started/first-boot.mdx#serial-console).

J2 is a battery connector, not a CR1220 coin-cell holder. The schematic labels the external RTC as PCF85263ATL; Linux reports the compatible driver name `pcf85363`. Driver naming alone does not establish a board revision. Battery retention was not tested in this review.

## Other connectors

| Interface | Board connection | Integration note |
|---|---|---|
| Power | USB Type-C, 5 V | See [Power & thermal](./power-and-thermal.mdx) |
| Ethernet | Gigabit RJ45, DP83867 PHY | Transformer centre taps are brought out on J5 for a PoE HAT |
| PoE header J5 | 2 × 2, 2.54 mm, next to the RJ45 | Standard 4-pin PoE HAT header: 1 `TR1_TAP`, 2 `TR0_TAP`, 3 `TR3_TAP`, 4 `TR2_TAP` (schematic sheet 18). InHand has run the board from a PoE HAT made for this header; the HAT then supplies 5 V through header pins 2/4. Not repeated in this review; confirm pin 1 against the schematic before fitting a HAT |
| USB | Four USB 2.0 Type-A ports through a hub | Share the upstream link and power budget |
| Display | micro HDMI through SiI9022A | Use the tested [display setup](../mo-os/display-and-audio.mdx) |
| Audio | 3.5 mm jack with TLV320AIC3106 | Distinct from an optional header audio HAT |
| Antenna | U.FL-style RF connector | Fit the correct antenna and provide enclosure clearance |

The physical-pin mapping was cross-checked against the [SDK README](https://github.com/inhandnet/Mo62A#7-hardware-reference), the per-pad device-tree definitions, the individual overlays and the published schematics. The diagram's logical mapping was checked for all 40 pins. Only the GPIO, UART5 and pin-33 PWM loopbacks linked above are electrical tests; the remaining entries are design references.
