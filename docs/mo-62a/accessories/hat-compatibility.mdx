## A 40-pin connector is not a compatibility guarantee

No complete 40-pin HAT add-on was electrically or mechanically qualified in this
documentation review. The board exposes a 40-pin header, but signal mapping,
pin multiplexing, drivers and mounting clearances must be checked for each
add-on. The BCM-style GPIO numbers printed on many HATs are not Mo62A Linux GPIO offsets.

:::warning[Power off before changing the assembly]
Shut down and disconnect power before fitting a HAT or changing jumpers. Mo62A
GPIO uses 3.3 V logic. Do not connect a 5 V signal or two driven outputs, and do
not back-power the board from a HAT while USB-C is connected.
:::

## Compare physical pins

The original schematics identify the expansion header as J6. Locate physical
pin 1 on the actual board: the illustration below is a logical reference, not
a photograph showing connector orientation.

</a>

| Function needed by the add-on | Mo62A physical pins | Required check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I²C control | 3 SDA, 5 SCL | `microSD-i2c0`; check address, pull-up voltage and driver |
| UART | 8 TX, 10 RX | `microSD-uart5`; 3.3 V TTL, not RS-232 |
| SPI | 19 MOSI, 21 MISO, 23 clock, 24/26 chip select | `microSD-spi0`; check mode, frequency and device binding |
| PWM | 33 EHRPWM0 A, 32 B | `microSD-ehrpwm0`; only pin 33 was measured in the loopback |
| Audio | 12, 35, 38, 40; 3/5 control | `microSD-audio` is specific to the supplied WM8960 configuration |
| Pins 27/28 | Camera I²C bus | Reserved on Mo62A; do not assume HAT EEPROM discovery is available |
| PWM alternate function on pin 36 | EHRPWM1 A | Shares the fan's controller; not an independent tested PWM channel |

The complete GPIO controller/offset mapping and schematic are in
[Hardware interfaces](../hardware-development/hardware-interfaces.mdx).
Do not install device-tree overlays or configuration tools written for other
single-board computers as a substitute for a matching Mo62A device tree.

## What the existing tests establish

Three wire pairs were tested: 7↔11, 8↔10 and 33↔35. GPIO passed in both
directions; UART5 passed text and all 256 byte values at 115200 baud; pin 33 PWM
was measured through pin 35 at 50 Hz and 25% duty.

<a href={gpioResult} target="_blank" rel="noreferrer"><img src={gpioResult} alt="Real GPIO loopback test for three Mo62A pin pairs, not a complete HAT qualification" /></a>

These are local loopbacks, not HAT driver, bus-loading, voltage-margin or
mechanical tests. External I²C devices and SPI transfers were not tested in
this review. The presence of a WM8960 overlay does not establish that a
particular audio HAT works.

Use [GPIO & peripherals](../application-development/gpio-and-peripherals.mdx)
for the scripts and cleanup. Do not enable the audio overlay with those
loopback jumpers still attached; pin 35 changes function. Remove jumpers only
after shutting down and disconnecting power.

## Validate one add-on at a time

1. Record the exact HAT model, revision, schematic and power requirements.
2. Compare all used pins, pull-ups, chip selects and any EEPROM wiring.
3. Check mounting holes, standoffs, underside components and connector access
   against the physical Mo62A assembly; nominal board dimensions are not enough.
4. Confirm an appropriate Mo62A kernel driver and overlay. Select a documented
   boot entry and retain a known-working card for recovery.
5. With the correct wiring and power budget, verify detection, actual
   read/write operation, cleanup and recovery from failure.
6. Record image/kernel versions and results. Qualify simultaneous peripherals
   separately; individual tests do not prove a combined setup.

An unknown pin assignment, supply path or driver is a reason to stop and obtain
the missing information, not to try a random overlay.
